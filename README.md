# CS250 Portfolio

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective from CS 250.

## Reflection

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?

Throughout the SNHU Travel project, I learned that understanding user needs starts with figuring out what users are trying to accomplish and what problems they need the program to solve. User stories helped turn stakeholder requirements into specific features the team could build by focusing on what the user wanted to accomplish and why it mattered. For example, features such as destination recommendations and budget filters were designed around the needs of travelers rather than technical requirements alone.

User stories also helped keep the development process organized and user-focused. Acceptance criteria provided clear expectations for what a completed feature should do, which made development and testing more effective. By using user stories, the team was able to prioritize the features that were most useful to travelers while ensuring the final product met stakeholder expectations.


### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach software development by breaking large projects into smaller pieces that are easier to manage. Throughout this course, I learned that planning, feedback, and continuous improvement help create a better final product. Instead of trying to build everything at once, I like working through projects one step at a time so I can evaluate my progress and make improvements along the way. The Scrum framework showed me the value of delivering functionality incrementally and making adjustments based on feedback throughout development.

In future development work, I hope to continue using Agile practices such as sprint planning, backlog prioritization, regular reviews, and retrospectives. These practices helped keep the project organized and allowed the team to adapt when requirements changed. I also appreciate the emphasis Agile places on communication and collaboration because it helps keep development aligned with user needs and project goals.


### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

A good team member communicates openly, collaborates effectively, and remains accountable for their work. During this course, I learned the importance of sharing information, providing constructive feedback, and supporting teammates in achieving sprint goals. Effective communication helps prevent misunderstandings and keeps everyone working toward the same objectives.

I also believe being a good team member means being reliable, willing to learn from others, and adaptable when challenges arise. Software development is rarely an individual effort, and successful projects depend on trust, transparency, and cooperation among team members. I know I still have a lot to learn, but I think being dependable, communicating with the team, and being willing to help others are just as important as writing good code.
